# Employee Wellbeing, Health Support & Preventive Retention Guidelines

## 1. Objective & Human-Centric Philosophy
Absenteeism is often an operational symptom of underlying personal, occupational, or socioeconomic challenges. The apparel industry thrives when workers are physically healthy, emotionally supported, and fairly treated.

## 2. High-Risk Absenteeism Proactive Interventions
When an employee is identified by the analytics system as having a **High Absenteeism Risk** or high historical leave utilization, HR should consider the following recommended interventions:

1. **Workplace Health Assessment**:
   - Offer an appointment with the factory Occupational Health Center (OHC).
   - Check for ergonomic strain, chronic respiratory complaints, or occupational fatigue, particularly in heavy fabric handling and pressing sections.

2. **Shift & Commute Flexibility**:
   - Assess if night shift schedules or transport route bottlenecks (e.g., long bus transit after overtime) are causing chronic exhaustion.
   - Consider temporarily reallocating the employee to day shift or adjacent sewing lines closer to their team mentor.

3. **Confidential Counseling & EAP (Employee Assistance Program)**:
   - Provide access to internal welfare officers or external professional counseling for personal stress, childcare burdens, or financial distress.

4. **Multi-Skilling & Buffer Planning for Production Supervisors**:
   - For sewing lines with clusters of high-risk employees (e.g. during monsoon seasons or post-festival periods), supervisors should cross-train buffer tailors to prevent line bottlenecks without forcing excessive overtime on remaining operators.
